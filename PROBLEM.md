# Problem

People treat their music library like a personal collection.

In Apple Music, that collection may include saved songs, albums, favorite
artists, playlists, playlist order, and years of small choices. When a user
misses a payment or cancels for a while, they may fear that this collection
will disappear or become hard to recover.

The pain is not only losing access to songs. The bigger pain is losing the
memory of what was in the library.

## Who Has This Problem

- Apple Music users with large libraries.
- People who rely heavily on playlists.
- Users who switch subscriptions on and off.
- People who worry about billing issues.
- Users who want a backup before canceling Apple Music.
- Music fans who care about keeping a personal record of their taste.

## What Existing Tools Do

Some tools already help with parts of this:

- Apple Music Sync Library keeps music synced while the subscription is active.
- Apple Music and iTunes can export some library data from desktop apps.
- SongShift, Soundiiz, TuneMyMusic, and FreeYourMusic help move playlists
  between services.
- Apple has some transfer tools for specific destinations.

These are useful, but they mostly focus on syncing, exporting, or moving music.
They do not feel like a simple insurance policy for an Apple Music library.

## What Is Missing

Users need something that says:

"Here is a safe copy of your library map. If something changes, you can see
what changed and rebuild what we can."

That means the app should save:

- Songs.
- Albums.
- Playlists.
- Playlist order.
- Track IDs where available.
- ISRC codes where available.
- Song title, artist, album, and duration.
- Snapshot dates.
- Restore status.

## The Hard Part

The hard part is not just making a list.

The hard part is rebuilding the library later. Songs can disappear from the
catalog, show up under a different version, have regional limits, or be matched
incorrectly.

The product should expect imperfect recovery and guide the user through it in
plain language.

## Our Product Promise

We should promise backup, clarity, and practical recovery help.

We should not promise perfect restoration of every song.

The honest promise is:

"We help you keep a personal backup of your Apple Music library and rebuild as
much of it as Apple Music allows."
